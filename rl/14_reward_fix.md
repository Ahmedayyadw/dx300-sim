## 14a. Corrected reward

Lab 13 reward per control step: `r = -(e^2 + 0.01 omega^2 + 0.03 P / P_REF)`. Corrected reward:

```
r = -(e^2 + 0.01 omega^2 + 20 P / P_REF + 17.5 h) / 50
h = max(0, |e| - 5 cm / L_boom)  if |omega| < 0.01 rad/s,  else 0
```

Chosen on the 20 tuning scenarios x 4 variants only (`rl/14_reward_probe.py`, `rl/14_reward_probe.csv`):

- A larger energy weight alone cannot rank economical > balanced > fast: economical > balanced needs
  w > 2.42, but above w = 0.61 the economical PID told to stop 20 cm short of the target scores better
  than the one stopping 10 cm short, and above w = 0.95 better than the fast PID. The e^2 term charges
  mainly the approach, where the fast PID wins; a small error at the end costs almost nothing.
- The stall term h charges an error over the tolerance only while the boom is (nearly) stopped, so it
  hits stopping short and not a slow approach. With it the order holds for w >= 4.2 (stall speed
  0.01 rad/s); w = 20 is where the smallest margin stops growing. The scale 1/50 puts the economical
  return on the tuning set at the Lab 13 value.
- The margin is thin: on the tuning set the mean return of the economical PID is 0.7 % above the
  balanced one, and the order holds in 42 % of the single episodes.

## Statement

**No Lab 14 controller beats the economical PID** (0.00 % misses over 20 cm, 2.1 cm mean error, 159.1 kJ E_mech). **The gap does not close.**
Closest Lab 14 controller (fewest misses over 20 cm, then least E_mech): BC, economical-PID data only (500k), 0.09 % misses, 4.4 cm mean error, 157.6 kJ (-1.5 kJ against the economical PID).

Training objective: SAC 100k, corrected reward; TD3 100k, corrected reward; IQL 1M, corrected reward; CQL 1M, corrected reward; SAC 100k, corrected reward, lr x0.3 (1 seed); SAC 100k, corrected reward, lr x3 (1 seed); TD3 100k, corrected reward, lr x0.3 (1 seed); TD3 100k, corrected reward, lr x3 (1 seed) reach a higher corrected return than the economical PID (-4.699) on the tuning scenarios.

What this means for the reward-mismatch explanation:

- Online (SAC, TD3): the learners did optimise the corrected reward (higher return than the economical PID), but the reward could be exploited. E_mech fell below the PID (136.0 and 134.8 kJ) because the boom does not finish the move: 48.1 and 48.7 % misses over 20 cm. The learning-rate check (x0.3, x3, 1 seed) gives 30.2 to 52.8 % misses; no setting changes the verdict.
- Offline (IQL, CQL on the same data): the corrected reward moved them toward less energy (IQL 164.1 to 159.1 kJ, CQL 190.8 to 150.5 kJ) and lowered their accuracy (IQL 2.4 to 3.1 cm, misses over 20 cm 0.02 to 0.47 %). IQL reaches the PID energy, not its accuracy; CQL still misses 10.6 %.
- Data (14c): BC on the economical-PID data alone gives 157.6 kJ (-1.5 kJ) at 4.4 cm mean error (+2.2 cm, p <1e-300) and 0.09 % misses: a near copy of the PID, slightly cheaper and less accurate. The data supports the PID; a cloned PID does not beat it.
- A reward that ranks the PID points in the intended order was not enough. With a per-step trade of energy
  against error, the online learners traded accuracy for energy instead of finding the economical PID.
  The Lab 13 conclusion (no learner beats the economical PID) holds in this test. Not tested: a reward or
  constraint that cannot be paid for by missing (for example a hard accuracy constraint).
