Reading of the table (numbers of this run):

- Online learners (PPO, SAC, TD3) move the valve command 4 to 32 times more than the economical PID (mean abs(du/dt) 2.23 to 15.96 against 0.50). Their energy above the floor is 51.7 to 186.1 kJ against 26.6 kJ; 55 to 70 % of it is oscillation.
- Their error does not come from the sensor noise: without noise it is already 5.5 to 14.2 cm (economical PID 1.4 cm), and from 0 to 1 deg it changes by 0.8 cm at most (economical PID +2.6 cm). The residual itself moves the boom off the target.
- BC, 1M transitions: mean abs(du/dt) 0.55, the smoothness of the PID points (0.50 and 0.64). E_mech 162.6 kJ lies between the economical (159.1 kJ) and the balanced PID (167.5 kJ), the two behaviour policies of the dataset.
- IQL, 1M transitions: mean abs(du/dt) 0.55, the smoothness of the PID points (0.50 and 0.64). E_mech 164.1 kJ lies between the economical (159.1 kJ) and the balanced PID (167.5 kJ), the two behaviour policies of the dataset.
- CQL, 1M transitions: smooth command (mean abs(du/dt) 0.76), but 7.6 cm error without noise and 58.2 kJ above the floor, 56 % of it overshoot.
- Reward: the training reward at w = 0.03 ranks the balanced PID above the economical PID. Return per episode -9.78 against -10.30 without noise (probe on 300 training-distribution episodes, `docs/13_benchmark.md` section 3), -10.22 against -10.41 in the 1M dataset with noise (table above; the two behaviours ran on different episodes). A learner that maximises this reward is not pushed toward the economical PID. The return of the learned controllers was not measured.
